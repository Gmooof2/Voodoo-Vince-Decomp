# Relink a C++-EH function: place the __ehhandler$ stub and .xdata$x FuncInfo by reloc site, rename the next cut piece

Problem: our object for a C++-EH function (`_TRY_BEGIN`/catch, e.g. std `_Uninit_fill_n`) carries, beside the matched
`.text` COMDAT, a `.text$x` `__ehhandler$<fn>` stub (`mov eax, $T<n>; jmp ___CxxFrameHandler`) and a `.xdata$x`
FuncInfo/unwind/tryblock table (`$T<n>` statics, DIR32 to `$L<n>` labels in the function) plus `.sxdata`. `$T` names
are per-object compiler numbers: no symbol table gives their VA, and a global pin by name would collide.

Placement (2.3 T7.c2, tools/relink.py `_ours`, `_link`):
- Compute the `kept` closure (sections reachable by relocs from our matched functions) FIRST; only kept sections need
  holes (unkept COMDATs fall to /OPT:REF).
- A kept section with no VA by name gets it from a reloc site in an already-placed section of ours, to fixpoint:
  DIR32 VA = retail u32 at the site's retail VA - our field addend - target symbol value; REL32 adds site + 4.
  Disagreeing sites or none -> refuse. Stub VA came by name (`__ehhandler$...` in byname == retail field at fn+0x1);
  `.xdata$x` from the stub's `$T366` site.
- Where retail put them: every `.text$x` stub grouped at the END of .text (fixture stub 0x00152BDC, body ends
  0x00044BBD; C0072's "past the body" = this end group); `.xdata$x` near the end of .rdata (0x001FD8A8). LINK 7.10
  merges `.xdata` into `.rdata` with no /MERGE flag.
- Trap: LINK sorts `$`-grouped contributions after EVERY plain-named piece of the output section. So the cut piece
  following our stub/table must be renamed to sort after it: `<group>~` (`.text$x~`, `.xdata$x~`; 9 chars -> COFF
  long section name `/<offset>`). Unrenamed: .text 5 / .rdata 6132 differing offsets. Code holes of a static stub get
  no /ORDER slot (stays in its `$x` group).
- Check after link: placed sections' VAs printed and compared (pin, else the reloc-site VA); any mismatch exits 1.
Limits: two of our stubs with retail stubs between them refuse (next lever: command-line order within `.text$x`);
two of our objects with one `$T` name collide in the after-link check by name.
